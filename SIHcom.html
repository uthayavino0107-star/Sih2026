That matches this bottom-up build: ₹81,280 of parts, ₹92,908 all-in — leaving ₹5,000–₹20,000 for installation, commissioning and site work inside your ₹0.8–1.1 lakh. This supersedes the ₹45,000–₹60,000 kiosk figure from the earlier deck, which is no longer consistent with an RO/UF + analyser design.
Five conflicts the revised slides introduce — resolve these before the pitch.
  1. Zero waste vs RO concentrate. Stage 1 is now RO/UF with a "Concentrate / Rejected" line in your own block diagram. Conventional RO rejects 30–50% of feed. Your proposal and website still claim "~100% recovery" and "eliminating conventional RO". Either show the recirculation loop and a bleed that still hits your recovery number, or restate the claim as UF-dominant with RO used only on high-TDS sources.
  2. Diagram contradicts its own legend. In the block diagram the media cylinder is labelled "Filter 1" and the membrane module (with concentrate/reject arrows) sits at "Filter 2" — but the stage legend below says STAGE 1 = RO UF and STAGE 2 = target media. Relabel the drawing.
  3. The arsenic analyser is a batch test, not an online sensor. Reagent + colour development + optical read is periodic (every N litres or hours), so it cannot be the real-time interlock. Say the sampling interval explicitly and show what happens to the quality gate between samples. For context, a commercial online arsenic analyser is ₹5–15 lakh — your colorimetric route is the right call, but its duty cycle must be stated.
  4. Chlorine dose point vs membrane. Free chlorine oxidises As(III) → As(V) for the GFO/GFH bed, but it also degrades polyamide RO membranes. If you dose upstream of RO you must dechlorinate before the membrane — which your own flow puts the carbon filter after. Re-order the train: oxidise → adsorb → dechlorinate → (RO if needed) → UV, or dose downstream of RO.
  5. Nothing measures residual chlorine. You dose chlorine and then dechlorinate, with no free-chlorine reading at the outlet. Added to the BOM below as a DPD kit; make it a PASS/FAIL line on the dashboard alongside arsenic.
